FLIGHT PLAN — what this company is, in six parts:
  Mission: We will put HALF SOUR behind 83 New Zealand bar lists and in 2,000 New Zealand kitchens by 31 August 2028, because a drink made at home deserves the same ingredient a bar would reach for, because a one-person company can own a category a large one will not bother to enter, and because every bottle is still made by the person whose name is on it.
  Guiding principles, and the argument each one settles:
    - Culinary, never clinical. — settles: Every copy question. If a sentence would sit comfortably in a cookbook it ships; if it would sit comfortably in a pharmacy it does not, whatever it would do for the numbers.
    - The bar list before the billboard. — settles: Where the next spare hour goes. One venue account is 24 bottles a year and it advertises itself on a printed list; an hour of posting is neither.

    - One decision, written down. — settles: What the AI team may do alone. Anything a customer could see waits for a human, and the reason is recorded next to the decision, so the same argument is not had twice.
  The range, and what each bottle is for:
    - No 01 HOUSE — The everyday one. The bottle that gets opened for cooking as well as drinking, which is what makes it the volume line.
    - No 02 DIRTY — One drink. Filtered clear so it will not cloud a stirred one, which is the specific failure it was built to fix.
    - No 03 SMOKE — Heat and smoke. The bottle that wins the taste test at a market stall and loses the reorder if the customer only drinks one style.
    - No 04 WILD — Seafood. The bottle that says the range has a top end, and the one that cannot be scaled.
    - The Full Set — Acquisition. Three bottles in one parcel is the only direct order shape whose courier cost is not eating the margin.
  Direct sales: The market model's year 2 target is 4,356 direct bottles, which at 2.4 bottles an order is about 151 orders a month — roughly five a day, all packed on Tuesday.
  Trade sales: The market model's year 2 target is 83 live accounts, which at a one-in-five close rate is 4 approaches a week for two years. That is Thursday, and it is why Thursday is protected.
  Hard constraint: Two production days a week is the hard ceiling. At 120 bottles a day and 44 working weeks that is 10,560 bottles a year, and no amount of marketing moves it.
