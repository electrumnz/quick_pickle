### Verdict: HALF SOUR is not blocked in New Zealand. Build on it.

Two conditions, both about _distinctiveness_ rather than conflict. An attorney signs these off
before filing; the identity in this document is already designed around them.

1. **"Half sour" is the trade term for the category.** Doc Pickle (US) sells a product literally
   named _Half Sour Pickle Brine_. Under s18(1)(b)–(c) Trade Marks Act 2002, a mark descriptive of
   the kind or quality of the goods can be refused registration.
   **Mitigation:** file the **composite** first — the drawn wordmark plus the Half device — as a
   logo mark. That is registrable as artwork on day one. Pursue the plain word mark on acquired
   distinctiveness after 18–24 months of trading. This is precisely why the wordmark in section 3
   is _drawn_, not typeset: a typeset name is not ownable; drawn letterforms are.
2. **File class 32 first** (non-alcoholic beverages and preparations for making beverages).
   That is the true class for a mixer and it is empty of conflicts. Class 33 can follow once
   there is a reason to be there; `SOURZ` sits in class 33 and is owned by Beam, who defend.

## 1. The name

**HALF SOUR** is the delicatessen term for a cucumber cured in salt brine rather than vinegar —
kept crisp, kept green, only partway to a full sour. It is a real word from the trade, which means
it does three things at once:

- It is **accurate**. This is brine, not vinegar. The name describes the process.
- It is **restrained**. It is not a pun. It never has to explain itself twice.
- It is **an adjective a bartender already owns**. "Half sour" is a taste description before it is
  a brand, and that is why it survives being printed small on a back-bar shelf next to Angostura.

The dry joke is that it also describes the brand's temperament: sour, but only halfway.
We never say that out loud.

**Backups, in order:** BRINE CLUB (clear, `.com` taken), DILLINGER (clear, but the Dillinger
association is heavier than the product wants).

**Rejected:** MOTHER BRINE — collides with Coca-Cola's _Mother_ in NZ/AU.

## 2. Positioning

> **HALF SOUR is a bottled pickle brine built as an ingredient in its own right — measured,
> consistent, and good enough to put on a menu.**

**The category truth we are moving on.** The 2026 pickle boom is a _bar_ trend, not a snack
trend. Dua Lipa's pickle-juice-in-Diet-Coke video moved it out of the deli and into the glass;
pickle mixer unit sales are up ~140%; over the last 52 weeks pickle-flavoured spirits grew 29% and
pickle mixers 24%, against ~1% for beverage alcohol and mixers overall. The mixer market is
US$0.92bn (2025) heading to US$2.01bn (2034). The formats that are actually growing are cocktails
and bartender-facing bottles — not snack jars, and not anything sold on function.

**What we are.** The brine. On purpose, in a bottle, with the spec printed on it.

**What we are not, and will not become:** a sports or recovery drink; a gut-health or probiotic
product; a novelty shot; a snack brand with a drink attached.

**Positioning statement, long form.**
For bartenders and cooks who already reach past the pickle for the jar, HALF SOUR is a New Zealand
pickle brine made to be poured rather than poured away. Unlike the brine you get free at the bottom
of a jar — which is inconsistent, cloudy and different every time — HALF SOUR is cured in fixed
batches to a fixed salinity so a drink built on it tastes the same in June as it did in March.

## 3. Target customer

**Primary — on-premise (the credibility engine).**
Bar managers and head bartenders in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch and Queenstown, plus small
restaurant kitchens. They buy 500ml and 2L. They care about: consistency batch to batch, pour cost,
whether it clouds a stirred drink, and whether the bottle looks acceptable on a back bar that
customers can see. They are the reason the label is set like a spirits label and not like a
condiment.

**Secondary — the informed home bar (the volume).**
27–42, cooks properly, owns a mixing glass and at least one bottle of vermouth they feel guilty
about. Found us because a bartender told them, or because they saw No. 02 in a photo of somebody
else's kitchen bench. Buys 2–3 bottles at a time and a variety box at Christmas.

**Tertiary — the gift and grocer channel.**
Independent grocers, providores and bottle shops. Buys because the shelf presence does work that
their other stock does not.

**Not our customer:** anyone buying it to feel better afterwards.

## 5. Voice

### Five principles

1. **Lead with the instruction.** The most useful sentence goes first. Charm is the second
   sentence, if there is room.
2. **Short declaratives. One idea per sentence.** If a sentence needs a comma to survive, cut it in
   two.
3. **The joke is a shrug, not a punchline.** Understatement, deadpan, an honest admission.
   Never a pun, never a wink, never an emoji doing the work a word should do.
4. **Specific beats superlative.** "Three weeks in a 400-litre tank" outperforms "artisan" every
   time. Numbers are the whole personality.
5. **Own the unglamorous moment.** Out-of-stock notices, error pages and shipping emails are where
   most brands drop the voice. They are where ours is loudest, because nobody expects it there.

### What HALF SOUR never says

- **Anything about the body.** No electrolytes, hydration, cramps, recovery, probiotics, gut health,
  immunity, "good for you", "packed with", "supports", "natural energy". Not as a claim, not as a
  joke, not in a customer testimonial we choose to repost. This is FSANZ / MPI territory and it is
  the fastest way to turn a brand into a compliance problem. **Zero tolerance, all channels.**
- **Puns.** No "Dill With It", no "Big Dill", no "Kind of a Big Dill". Ever.
- **Kiwiana.** No jandals, no fern, no #8 wire, no "sweet as", no L&P-adjacent nostalgia,
  no kiwi bird, no tiki, no "Aotearoa" as decoration. We say _Made in Aotearoa New Zealand_ on the
  label because it is a fact of manufacture, and nowhere else.
- **Retro deli.** No 1950s script, no checkerboard, no sunburst, no "since 19—", no faux-heritage
  crest, no distressed paper. The category has done this to death.
- **Manufactured urgency.** No countdown timers, no "only 3 left!!" when there are thirty, no
  "drops". Stock numbers are real or they are absent.
- **Superlatives.** No "best", "world-class", "award-winning" (until there is an award, and then
  once, in small type).
- **Wellness register.** No "clean", "pure", "wholesome", "mindful", "ritual", "nourish".
- **Exclamation marks.** One per year, and it had better be worth it.

### Colour rules

1. **Brine is a field, not an ink** — on light grounds. Brine type on Bone is 1.36:1 and is
   forbidden. Put Ink _on_ Brine instead. On dark grounds Brine 600 may be used as type.
2. **One Brine event per view.** A cap, a band, a highlight — one. Two makes it a sports drink.
3. **Ink is never `#000`.** Pure black next to Bone reads cheap.
4. **Chilli is reserved.** Errors, sold-out states, allergen notices, and No. 03. Never decorative.
5. **No gradients** except the physical one on glass in a product render.

## 8. Typography

Three voices and a fourth that is ours.

### HALF SOUR Display — the drawn face

The wordmark, the pack names and the poster headlines are set in a **display alphabet drawn for
this brand**: 39 characters (A–Z, 0–9, `/`, `-`, `·`), monolinear, cap-only, cap height 120,
stem 21, flat radial terminals, low crossbars, condensed round forms built on vertical stadiums.
It exists as parametric geometry (`brand/_src/letterforms.py`), so every asset in `brand/assets/`
is generated from the same source of truth and no asset contains live text.

**Use it for:** the wordmark, product names on pack, numerals on pack, poster headlines, the
descriptor rule. **Never for:** body copy, anything below 11px, anything with lowercase.

### Pairing rules

1. **One serif moment per screen.** Instrument Serif carries the headline. It never appears in the
   same block as another Instrument Serif headline.
2. **Mono earns its place by being information.** If a mono line is not a number, a label, a code
   or a category, it should be Archivo.
3. **Never track body copy.** Tracking is a signal reserved for Data and Micro. Tracked body copy
   reads as a wellness brand.
4. **Measure: 62–72 characters.** Wider and the Archivo loses its rhythm.
5. **HS Display never mixes inline** with another face inside a sentence. It is a block element.
6. **Numbers in Martian Mono, always** — prices, volumes, batch numbers, dates, stock counts.
   Numbers are the brand's whole personality; give them their own face.

| **No. 01** | **HOUSE** | Dill and Peppercorn Brine | The everyday brine — dill, garlic, black peppercorn, coriander seed. Salty, clean, faintly sweet at the finish. |
| **No. 02** | **DIRTY** | Martini Brine             | Cut heavier with salt and coriander seed, filtered clear so it won't cloud a stirred drink.                     |
| **No. 03** | **SMOKE** | Smoked Chilli Brine       | Smoked chilli, bay and garlic, for the margarita, the michelada, and anything that wanted hot sauce.            |

Formats: **500ml** flint glass for retail and the home bar; **2L** for on-premise.

### Label art direction

The label is set like a **spirits label, not a condiment label**. That single decision does most of
the strategic work: it is what lets a bar put the bottle where customers can see it.

- **Stock:** uncoated bone paper, wet-strength (it lives in a fridge and it will get wet), no
  varnish, no foil, no emboss. The only finish is the ink.
- **Architecture, top to bottom:** hairline rule → HALF SOUR wordmark → hairline rule → `PICKLE
BRINE` tracked to 0.44em → the Half seal in the SKU colour → the SKU number in a hairline box →
  the product name at 62pt cap → the descriptor → the ingredient run, slashed, in Pepper →
  a solid foot band carrying `500 ML` and `MADE IN AOTEAROA NEW ZEALAND`.
- **The variable is the foot band and the seal.** No. 01 is Brine, No. 02 is Ink, No. 03 is Chilli.
  Nothing else changes between SKUs — same grid, same type sizes, same everything. A range that
  changes only its colour block reads as a _range_; a range that redesigns itself per flavour reads
  as three unrelated products.
- **The cap matches the band.** It is the only thing visible from three metres away.
- **Everything is centred.** Centred type on a tall label is formal. Formal is the joke.
- **Never on the label:** a cucumber illustration, a jar, a splash graphic, a mascot, a script
  face, a starburst, a medal, a "new!" flash, any claim about what the product does to a person.

Artwork: `assets/label-01-house.svg`, `label-02-dirty.svg`, `label-03-smoke.svg`.
On-bottle: `assets/bottles.svg`.

## 10. Photography and art direction

**The one-sentence brief:** _a HALF SOUR photograph looks like a bar at 4pm — hard light, real
glass, nothing styled that isn't going into a drink._

### What it is

|                      |                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Light**            | One hard source, high and to one side, 45°–70°. Deep, sharp, honest shadows with a defined edge. Ratio around 8:1. No fill on the shadow side except what the surface gives you.       |
| **Surface**          | Dark: honed stone, stainless bench, a wet bar top, zinc. Or Bone: a plain sheet of the label stock. Never wood grain, never marble, never linen.                                       |
| **Palette in frame** | Ink, Bone, Brine, glass, steel, and one green. If a colour is in the shot and not in the palette, it leaves.                                                                           |
| **Subject**          | The bottle, the pour, the glass, the ingredient. Cucumber in cross-section, dill fronds, whole peppercorns, a garlic clove, a chilli — placed like tools, not scattered like confetti. |
| **Liquid**           | Always in motion or freshly still. A pour caught mid-stream. Condensation on a coupe. A meniscus. Brine is cloudy and we show that; we never fake clarity.                             |
| **Human**            | Hands only, and working ones — a jigger, a bar spoon, a wrist. No faces, no lifestyle, no laughing.                                                                                    |
| **Crop**             | Tight and slightly uncomfortable. Cut the bottle. Let things leave the frame.                                                                                                          |
| **Grain**            | A little. Digital-clean reads like stock photography.                                                                                                                                  |

### What it is never

- **Never a lifestyle shot.** No picnic, no group of friends, no beach, no deck, no barbecue.
- **Never kiwiana.** No ferns, no jandals, no bach, no landscape.
- **Never retro-deli.** No checkerboard, no red-and-white, no butcher paper, no chalkboard, no
  1950s counter.
- **Never wellness.** No gym, no towel, no water bottle, no post-workout anything, no supplement
  shelf, no white studio infinity curve, no "clean" flat-lay on white marble with eucalyptus.
- **Never a mascot.** No anthropomorphised anything.
- **Never a splash.** No frozen-splash-with-fruit-suspended-in-it stock cliché.
- **Never warm and cosy.** No golden hour, no candlelight, no soft focus, no bokeh hearts.
- **Never over-propped.** If you can count more than five objects in the frame, remove two.

### The three plates

1. **Bar-side.** Bottle at the edge of frame, coupe centre, brine going in from a jigger.
   Dark stone, hard light from the right, long shadow left. Cropped so the bottle is half out.
2. **Ingredient.** Overhead, Bone ground, cucumber cross-sections, dill, peppercorns, one garlic
   clove and a shallow pool of brine, arranged on an invisible grid. Hard light, no fill.
   This is the shot that proves it is food, not a drink product.
3. **Bench.** A chicken in a stainless bowl, brine being poured over it, one hand. Kitchen light,
   still hard. This is the shot that proves the culinary half of the positioning and keeps us out
   of the mixer-only box.

_Rendered plates were not producible in this build — see section 12._

**Every asset is outlined geometry. There is no live `<text>` element in any file in
`brand/assets/`** — verified by grep. Every file carries `role="img"`, an `aria-label` and a
`<title>`. `guide.html` makes **zero external requests**: the three web faces are embedded as
base64 woff2, every SVG is inlined, and the favicon and avatar are `data:` URIs. It renders with
no horizontal overflow at 390, 768, 1024 and 1600px.

### Logo rules

- **Clear space:** the cap height of the wordmark on all four sides. Nothing enters it.
- **Minimum sizes:** wordmark 96px wide on screen / 24mm in print. Mark 16px (use `favicon.svg`
  below 24px). Stacked lockup 120px wide.
- **The Half is never rotated.** The fill is always the left half. It is a cut cucumber, not a moon
  phase.
- **Approved colourways:** Ink on Bone, Ink on Paper, Ink on Brine, Bone on Ink, Bone on Dill,
  Brine 600 on Night. Nothing else.
- **Never:** stretch, outline, add a keyline, add a shadow, place on a photograph without a solid
  field behind it, recreate the wordmark in a font, or set the descriptor in anything but the drawn
  alphabet.

## 12. Build notes and known gaps

- **Trade mark search route.** IPONZ's own endpoint refused non-browser clients (403) and this
  environment's Chromium had no network egress, so the register was read through TMview, which
  carries IPONZ data. A pre-filing search by a New Zealand attorney is still required — TMview
  lags the live register by days and does not surface pending oppositions.
- **Photographic plates were not produced.** Image generation was unavailable in this environment:
  the MCP image tool returned `invoke operation is disabled because gradio=none is set`, and the
  Hugging Face Spaces backing it (`mcp-tools/Qwen-Image`, `mcp-tools/Qwen-Image-Fast`) returned
  `event: error` on every direct Gradio API call — ZeroGPU requires an authenticated token that is
  not present here. `mcp-tools/FLUX.1-Krea-dev` and `evalstate/flux1_schnell` were returning HTTP
  503 (space in error) throughout. Section 10 is therefore written as an enforceable specification
  rather than a mood board, and the product renders in `assets/bottles.svg` are authored vector,
  not photography. The three plates in §10.3 are the shot list for the first photo day.
- **Not in scope for v1.0:** motion, sound, packaging engineering (closure liner, fill line,
  wet-strength label adhesive), on-premise POS, or the 2L format artwork.
