Condition 01 — distinctiveness

“Half sour” is the trade term for the category

Doc Pickle (US) sells a product literally named Half Sour Pickle Brine. Under s18(1)(b)–(c) Trade Marks Act 2002 a mark descriptive of the kind or quality of the goods can be refused. Mitigation: file the composite first — the drawn wordmark plus the Half device — as a logo mark, which is registrable as artwork on day one. Pursue the plain word mark on acquired distinctiveness after 18–24 months of trading. This is precisely why the wordmark in §09 is drawn rather than typeset: a typeset name is not ownable; drawn letterforms are.

Condition 02 — filing order

Class 32 first. Class 33 can wait.

Class 32 (non-alcoholic beverages and preparations for making beverages) is the true class for a mixer, and it is empty of conflicts — zero hits for both half sour and brine. Class 33 contains SOURZ (reg. 295846), owned by Jim Beam Brands Co., who defend. There is no reason to walk into that until there is a reason to be in class 33 at all.

What we are

The brine. On purpose, in a bottle, with the spec printed on it. Cured in fixed batches to a fixed salinity, so a drink built on it tastes the same in June as it did in March — which is the one thing the free brine at the bottom of a jar can never promise.

What we will not become

A sports or recovery drink. A gut-health or probiotic product. A novelty shot. A snack brand with a drink attached. Each of those is a larger short-term market and a smaller long-term business, and two of them are regulatory problems.

03

Customer

One buyer creates the credibility, another creates the volume
Primary — on-premise

The credibility engine

Bar managers and head bartenders in Auckland, Wellington, Christchurch and Queenstown, plus small restaurant kitchens. They buy 500ml and 2L. They care about consistency batch to batch, pour cost, whether it clouds a stirred drink, and whether the bottle is acceptable on a back bar customers can see. They are the reason the label is set like a spirits label.

Secondary — home bar

The volume

27–42, cooks properly, owns a mixing glass and at least one bottle of vermouth they feel guilty about. Found us because a bartender told them, or because No. 02 was in a photo of somebody else’s kitchen bench. Buys two or three bottles at a time and a variety box at Christmas.

Tertiary — trade

The shelf

Independent grocers, providores and bottle shops. Buys because the shelf presence does work their other stock does not. Reorders on sell-through, not on story.

Not our customer: anyone buying it to feel better afterwards.

04

Personality

Five traits, each with the thing it must not tip into

Exact

Numbers are real. Batch sizes, cure times, salinity, stock counts. If we print it, it is true.

Clinical. We are not a lab report.

Dry

The humour lives in restraint and timing, never in wordplay. We under-react.

Jokey. We never do a bit.

Useful

Every piece of copy leaves you knowing what to do with the bottle.

Preachy. We do not lecture people about food.

Unbothered

We do not oversell, count down, or manufacture scarcity. If it is gone, it is gone.

Aloof. We answer emails like a person.

Appetising

It has to sound like it tastes good. Salt, dill, snap, cold glass.

Gushing. No “obsessed”, no “insane”, no exclamation marks.

The most serious person at the bar, who is also, quietly, the funniest.

Voice

Five principles. Twenty real lines. The unglamorous ones matter most.
01

Lead with the instruction

The most useful sentence goes first. Charm is the second sentence, if there is room.

02

Short declaratives

One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a comma to survive, cut it in two.

03

The joke is a shrug

Understatement, deadpan, an honest admission. Never a pun, never a wink, never an emoji doing a word’s job.

04

Specific beats superlative

“Three weeks in a 400-litre tank” outperforms “artisan” every time. Numbers are the whole personality.

Own the unglamorous moment

Out-of-stock notices, error pages and shipping emails are where most brands drop the voice. It is where ours is loudest.

What HALF SOUR never says

Scan this list before anything ships

Anything about the body

No electrolytes, hydration, cramps, recovery, probiotics, gut health, immunity, “good for you”, “supports”. Not as a claim, not as a joke, not in a customer post we repost. FSANZ / MPI. Zero tolerance, all channels.

Puns

No “Dill With It”. No “Big Dill”. No “Kind of a Big Dill”. Ever.

Kiwiana

No jandals, fern, #8 wire, “sweet as”, kiwi bird, tiki. Made in Aotearoa New Zealand appears on the label because it is a fact of manufacture, and nowhere else.

Retro deli

No 1950s script, checkerboard, sunburst, “since 19—”, faux-heritage crest, distressed paper. The category has done this to death.

Manufactured urgency

No countdown timers. No “only 3 left!!” when there are thirty. No “drops”. Stock numbers are real or absent.

Superlatives

No “best”, “world-class”, “award-winning” — until there is an award, and then once, in small type.

Wellness register

No “clean”, “pure”, “wholesome”, “mindful”, “ritual”, “nourish”.

Exclamation marks

One per year, and it had better be worth it.

Label artwork — flat, at size

Label direction

Set like a spirits label, not a condiment label

That one decision does most of the strategic work: it is what lets a bar put the bottle where customers can see it. Stock: uncoated bone paper, wet-strength — it lives in a fridge and it will get wet. No varnish, no foil, no emboss. The only finish is the ink. Architecture, top to bottom: hairline rule → wordmark → hairline rule → PICKLE BRINE at 0.44em → the Half seal in the SKU colour → the SKU number in a hairline box → the name at 62pt cap → the descriptor → the ingredient run, slashed, in Pepper → a solid foot band carrying 500 ML and MADE IN AOTEAROA NEW ZEALAND.

The variable

Only the band and the seal change

No. 01 is Brine, No. 02 is Ink, No. 03 is Chilli. Nothing else moves between SKUs — same grid, same type sizes, same everything. A range that changes only its colour block reads as a range; a range that redesigns itself per flavour reads as three unrelated products. The cap matches the band, because from three metres the cap is the only thing you can see. Everything is centred, because centred type on a tall label is formal, and formal is the joke. Never on the label: a cucumber illustration, a jar, a splash, a mascot, a script face, a starburst, a medal, a “new!” flash, or any claim about what the product does to a person.

Art direction

A bar at four in the afternoon

Hard light, real glass, and nothing styled that isn’t going into a drink.

What it is

Eight non-negotiables
Light

One hard source, high and to one side, 45°–70°. Deep, sharp shadows with a defined edge. Ratio around 8:1. No fill except what the surface gives you.

Surface

Honed stone, stainless bench, a wet bar top, zinc. Or a plain sheet of the label stock. Never wood grain, never marble, never linen.

Palette in frame

Ink, Bone, Brine, glass, steel, and one green. If a colour is in the shot and not in the palette, it leaves.

Subject

The bottle, the pour, the glass, the ingredient. Cucumber in cross-section, dill, whole peppercorns, a garlic clove — placed like tools, not scattered like confetti.

Liquid

Always in motion or freshly still. A pour caught mid-stream. Condensation on a coupe. Brine is cloudy and we show that.

Human

Hands only, and working ones — a jigger, a bar spoon, a wrist. No faces, no lifestyle, no laughing.

Crop

Tight and slightly uncomfortable. Cut the bottle. Let things leave the frame.

Grain

A little. Digital-clean reads like stock photography.

What it is never

Lifestyle

No picnic, no group of friends, no beach, no deck, no barbecue.

Kiwiana

No ferns, no jandals, no bach, no landscape.

Retro deli

No checkerboard, no red-and-white, no butcher paper, no chalkboard, no 1950s counter.

Wellness

No gym, no towel, no post-workout anything, no supplement shelf, no white infinity curve, no eucalyptus flat-lay.

Notes

Method, gaps, and what is not in v1.0

Trade mark route

IPONZ’s own endpoint refused non-browser clients with 403 and this environment’s Chromium had no network egress, so the register was read through TMview, which carries IPONZ data. A pre-filing search by a New Zealand attorney is still required.

Photographic plates were not produced
