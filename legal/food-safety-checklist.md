# HALF SOUR — Food Safety and Labelling Checklist

**For the founder.** This is the list of things that must physically happen — registrations, paperwork,
printed words on a bottle — before and while HALF SOUR is sold. Every requirement names its source.

**Status: none of this is optional, and some of it must happen before the first sale.**

## PART 1 — The operating licence: which risk-based measure applies

Under the **Food Act 2014 (NZ)** every food business must operate under a _risk-based measure_ — a
**food control plan (FCP)**, a **national programme (NP1/NP2/NP3)**, or an exemption — determined by
what the business actually does. **Schedule 2 of the Food Act 2014** lists the sectors subject to
national programmes.

**This is the single most consequential open question for HALF SOUR, because three different answers
are live and they carry different obligations.**

| If HALF SOUR is…                                                         | Sector                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Measure                        | Source                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| a **non-alcoholic beverage** — sold and positioned as a drink/mixer      | "Manufacturers of non-alcoholic beverages" — covers businesses that "prepare or manufacture carbonated or non-carbonated beverages, and **fruit or vegetable beverages**"                                                                          | **National Programme level 3** | Food Act 2014 Sch 2 Pt 1 table; Pt 3                     |
| a **shelf-stable condiment** — an acidified, shelf-stable pickle product | "Manufacturers of shelf-stable condiments (including sauces, spreads, or preserves)" — "includes products that have been **acidified or preserved**, or have low water activity, and are shelf-stable", examples include "**pickles or relishes**" | **National Programme level 2** | Food Act 2014 Sch 2 Pt 1 table; Pt 4                     |
| a **non-shelf-stable sauce or dressing**                                 | "manufacturers of non-shelf-stable sauces, spreads, dips, soups, broths, gravies, or dressings"                                                                                                                                                    | **Food control plan**          | Food Act 2014 Sch 2, exclusions under both sectors above |

"**Shelf-stable**" is defined in **Schedule 2 Part 2**: the food "can be stored at room or ambient
temperature before its packaging is opened", and after opening "may or may not require refrigeration".

**What this means in practice.** The classification turns on two facts about your product, not on how
you market it:

1. **Is it shelf-stable before opening?** If it needs refrigeration unopened, you are heading toward
   a **food control plan**, which is the most demanding of the three.
2. **Is it a beverage or a condiment?** A brine sold to be poured into a glass reads as a vegetable
   beverage (NP3). The same liquid sold as a cooking ingredient reads as a shelf-stable condiment
   (NP2). HALF SOUR is deliberately sold as **both**.

⚠️ **ACTION 1 — before any further sales.** Run MPI's **"My Food Rules"** tool
(mpi.govt.nz/food-business), then **email MPI or your territorial authority's environmental health
officer with your actual process and product spec and get the classification in writing.** Where a
business spans two sectors, MPI's practice is generally that the higher-risk measure governs — but
**do not rely on that sentence**; get it confirmed. This determines your registration, your fees, and
how often a verifier visits.

### If it is a national programme

- **Register** with your **territorial authority** (your local council), not with MPI, for most
  businesses. Registration for a national programme runs for **2 years**, against **1 year** for a
  food control plan. ⚠️ _Confirm current fees and the registering authority with your council._
- **Verification.** A new business must be verified within **6 weeks** of registration (extendable by
  up to a further 6 weeks); an existing business within **6 months**. ⚠️ _Source: MPI guidance,
  reached via search rather than a fetched MPI page — confirm the exact frequency for your level with
  your verifier._
- You must keep records showing you are meeting the requirements of the national programme — the
  detail is set by the **Food Notice: Requirements for Food Control Plans and National Programmes**
  issued under the Food Act 2014. ⚠️ _Obtain the current Food Notice from MPI directly._

### If it is a food control plan

- Either a **template FCP** provided by MPI, or a **custom FCP** you write and have **evaluated** and
  registered. A brine business with an acidification step will very likely need the custom route,
  because the safety of the product depends on a validated process.
- Registration is **annual**, and verification frequency starts high and reduces with good
  performance.
- ⚠️ **Confirm with MPI which template, if any, covers a bottled brine.**

### Either way

- **The Food Act 2014 imposes a general duty**: food that is sold must be **safe and suitable**. No
  registration removes that duty, and no verifier's sign-off is a defence for selling unsafe food.
- ⚠️ **ACTION 2 — process validation.** If the product's safety depends on acidity, you need a
  **validated pH and process**, in writing, from a food technologist. For an acidified,
  ambient-stable vegetable product the usual benchmark is an equilibrium **pH at or below 4.6** —
  ⚠️ _this figure is stated here from general food-science practice and was NOT read from a fetched
  New Zealand regulatory source in this pass. It must be set and validated by a qualified food
  technologist against your actual recipe, fill temperature and container._ Get the validation report
  and keep it; your verifier will ask for it, and it is your defence if anything goes wrong.
- ⚠️ **ACTION 3 — water, cleaning, pest control, personal hygiene, allergen segregation, glass
  breakage policy.** These are ordinary GMP requirements your FCP or national programme records must
  cover. If you are producing in a domestic kitchen, say so to MPI and expect additional conditions.

## PART 2 — What must be printed on the bottle

The Food Standards Code applies in New Zealand because it is incorporated in or adopted under the
Food Act 2014 (Standard 1.1.1—3(1), Note 2 to Standard 1.2.7—2). A packaged food for retail sale
**must bear a label** with the information in **Standard 1.2.1—8(1)** (Standard 1.2.1—6(1)).

**Note on Chapter 3.** The Code's food safety standards (Chapter 3) and primary production standards
(Chapter 4) are **not** incorporated into New Zealand law (Standard 1.1.1—3(1) Note 1). New Zealand
food safety runs through the Food Act 2014 instead. **Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 — including all the
labelling standards below — do apply.**

### The mandatory label elements

| #   | Element                                       | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | Source                                                              |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Name of the food**                          | A prescribed name if one exists; otherwise "a name or description sufficient to indicate the **true nature** of the food". "HALF SOUR No 01 HOUSE" is a brand, not a name — the label needs something like _Pickle brine_ or _Cucumber brine_ as well.                                                                                                                                  | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(a); **Std 1.2.2—2**                                  |
| 2   | **Lot identification**                        | A batch/lot code that lets a specific production run be identified. This is the backbone of traceability and recall.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(b); **Std 1.2.2—3**                                  |
| 3   | **Name and address of the supplier**          | The **name and a business address in Australia or New Zealand**. A PO box is not a business address; a website is not an address.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(c); **Std 1.2.2—4**                                  |
| 4   | **Allergen declarations**                     | See §"Allergens" below.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(d); **Std 1.2.3—4 to 1.2.3—7**; **Schedule 9, S9—3** |
| 5   | **Statement of ingredients**                  | Every ingredient, in **descending order of ingoing weight** (Std 1.2.4—5). Note: added water that "forms part of broth, **brine** or syrup that is declared in the statement of ingredients or is part of the name of the food" need not be separately listed (Std 1.2.4—3(2)(c)(ii)) — get a food technologist to apply this correctly rather than guessing.                           | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(e); **Std 1.2.4—2, 1.2.4—3, 1.2.4—5**                |

Not required at all if the best-before date is 2 years or more out (Std 1.2.5—3(2)(a)).                                            | Std 1.2.1—8(1)(f); **Std 1.2.5—3, 1.2.5—5**                         |
| 7   | **Storage conditions and directions for use** | Required where specific storage conditions are needed for the food to keep until its date, or where directions must be followed for health or safety reasons. | 8   | **Nutrition information panel**               | Required. The panel must show servings per package, serving size in **mL**, and per serving and per 100 mL: **energy (kJ), protein, fat, saturated fat, carbohydrate, sugars, and sodium (mg)**. **And the rule that binds the marketing to the label: Standard 1.2.1—23.** "If this Code prohibits a
label on or relating to food from including a statement, information, a design or a representation, an
advertisement for that food must not include that statement, information, design or representation."
The website is an advertisement. See `legal/COPY-RULES.md`.

### Allergens — Plain English Allergen Labelling

**Standard 1.2.3—4** requires a declaration where a food listed in **Column 1 of the table to
S9—3** — or a derivative of one — is present as an ingredient, as an ingredient of a compound
ingredient, as a food additive or component of one, or as a **processing aid** or component of one.

The declarable foods are: **added sulphites at 10 mg/kg or more**; **barley, oats, rye** (where they
contain gluten); **wheat** (whether or not it contains gluten); the named **tree nuts** (almond,
Brazil nut, cashew, hazelnut, macadamia, pecan, pine nut, pistachio, walnut); **crustacea**; **egg**;
**fish**; **lupin**; **milk**; **mollusc**; **peanut**; **sesame seed**; **soybean**.

The form is prescribed and is not a matter of house style:

- The **required name** must appear in the **statement of ingredients**, in **bold**, in a font size
  at least equal to the surrounding ingredient text, **and** the label must carry a **summary
  statement** (Std 1.2.3—6(2), 1.2.3—7(1)).
- The summary statement must **begin with the word "Contains"**, then list the required names, and
  **contain no other words** (Std 1.2.3—7(3)).
- It must appear **in the same field of view as, and directly next to, the statement of ingredients**,
  distinctly separated from it, in the same typeface and size and in bold (Std 1.2.3—7(4)–(5)).

**Relevant to HALF SOUR specifically:**

- **Sulphites** — if any ingredient (a vinegar, a preservative, a dried spice) brings added sulphites
  to **10 mg/kg or more**, it must be declared as **sulphites**. Ask every supplier in writing.
- **Mustard seed and coriander seed** are common pickling spices and are **not** on the Australian and
  New Zealand declarable list — do not add a declaration the Code does not require, and do not assume
  a list from another country applies.
- **Kelp / seaweed** in No 04 WILD is not an allergen, but see the novel-food question below.
- ⚠️ **"May contain" statements are voluntary and are not a substitute for controlling cross-contact.**
  If you use one, it must be honest and based on an actual assessment.

### Product-specific issues

- **No 04 WILD — novel food status.** **Standard 1.5.1** defines a _novel food_ as a _non-traditional
  food_ that requires safety assessment, and a _non-traditional food_ includes "a food that does not
  have a history of human consumption in Australia or New Zealand". ⚠️ **Get a written answer from
  FSANZ or MPI on whether kawakawa and kelp, at your inclusion rates, in a bottled beverage, are
  novel foods — before WILD is sold.** If a food is a novel food it may only be sold if it is
  permitted in the Code. This is a stop/go question, not a paperwork question.
- **No 04 WILD — wild harvest.** If kawakawa is wild-harvested, you need documented permission from
  the landowner or hapū, a documented harvest location per batch, and a supplier specification. That
  is a food-safety traceability requirement and a provenance-claim requirement (`COPY-RULES.md`
  §3A Group G).
- **No 03 SMOKE — smoked chilli.** Smoke flavourings are regulated as food additives/flavourings.
  Confirm your ingredient is a permitted form and that any additive is declared correctly under
  Std 1.2.4—7.
- **Do not call it an electrolyte drink.** "Electrolyte drink" is a **prescribed name** and
  **Std 2.6.2—11(1)** requires a food sold as one to _be_ one, meeting the composition in
  2.6.2—11(2). This is a composition offence, not just a copy problem.

## PART 3 — Batch coding and traceability

Traceability is what turns a problem into a recall of 300 bottles instead of everything you have ever
made.

**Minimum system — do all of it:**

1. **A lot code on every bottle**, matching **Std 1.2.2—3**, that is printed or applied so it survives
   condensation and handling. Date-of-production-based codes are simplest (e.g. `H01-260827-01` =
   product, date, run).
2. **A batch record per run**: date, product, recipe version, every ingredient with its supplier and
   the **supplier's** lot number, water source, quantities, pH and temperature readings, the number of
   units filled, and who did it.
3. **One step forward, one step back.** For every batch you must be able to say what went into it and
   where every unit went. For online direct-to-consumer that is straightforward — the order record is
   the forward trace. Keep the link between order and batch.
4. **Retention samples.** Keep at least one sealed unit of every batch, stored as labelled, until
   after its date mark expires. It is the only way to test a complaint.
5. **A complaints log.** Date, customer, product, batch, symptom, what you did. This is also a
   Privacy Act record — see `privacy-policy.md` §6.
6. **A written recall procedure**, tested at least once. It must name who decides, who contacts MPI,
   how customers are contacted, and what the public notice says. **MPI must be notified of a food
   recall** — ⚠️ _confirm the current notification route and timeframe with MPI; the mpi.govt.nz
   recall pages could not be fetched during this pass._
7. **Records retention.** Keep batch and traceability records for at least **shelf life plus 12
   months**, and longer if your national programme or FCP says so.

## PART 4 — Standing obligations once trading

- **Never sell after a use-by date** (**Std 1.2.5—4**).
- **Re-check the label whenever the recipe, supplier or process changes.** A new vinegar supplier can
  change an allergen declaration; a new spice blend can change a characterising percentage.
- **Re-check the NIP when the recipe changes.** Use the FSANZ Nutrition Panel Calculator or a lab.
- **Keep verification current.** A lapsed registration means trading unlawfully.
- **Keep supplier specifications and allergen statements on file, in writing, and refresh them
  annually.**
- **Watch for Code amendments.** The added-sugar claim rules varied by **Proposal P1063** are in a
  transition period to **29 October 2028**; carbohydrate and sugar claim rules for alcoholic beverages
  changed on **13 August 2025** (Proposal P1049). Set an annual reminder to re-read
  `COPY-RULES.md` against the current Code.

## PART 5 — Australia, before the first Australian sale

- The **Food Standards Code is the same instrument in both countries** (Std 1.1.1—3(1)) — the label's
  Code content travels unchanged.
- **Country-of-origin labelling is mandatory in Australia** and is imposed by an information standard
  made under the Australian Consumer Law, not by the Code. The Code's own country-of-origin
  provisions were repealed for that reason (Std 1.2.1 amendment history, F2016L01367, effective
  1 July 2018). ⚠️ **Confirm with the ACCC whether pickle brine is a "priority" food — that decides
  whether a full bar-chart origin label is required.**
- **Chapter 3 food safety standards DO apply in Australia** even though they do not in New Zealand.
  ⚠️ Confirm what an importer/exporter must do, including **Standard 3.2.2A Food Safety Management
  Tools**.
- ⚠️ Confirm export/import requirements, biosecurity conditions for any plant ingredient (kawakawa and
  kelp especially), and who is the importer of record.

## The short list, in order

1. ⚠️ Get the **Food Act sector classification in writing** from MPI or your council. _(Blocks
   everything.)_
2. ⚠️ Get the **process validated** by a food technologist — pH, fill, shelf life. _(Blocks the date
   mark and the storage statement.)_
3. **Register** the FCP or national programme, and book the first verification.
4. ⚠️ Get the **novel-food question answered for No 04 WILD**. _(Blocks WILD's sale.)_
5. Have a food technologist or labelling consultant **check the artwork against Part 2** before the
   print run.
6. Get the **NIP** from the FSANZ calculator or a lab.
7. Collect **written allergen and sulphite statements** from every supplier.
8. Stand up **batch coding, batch records, retention samples and the recall procedure**.
9. Fill every placeholder in the other documents in this folder.
10. Set an **annual review** of this checklist and of `COPY-RULES.md`.
